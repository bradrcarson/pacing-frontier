Proposals to Stop

  • Pause Giant AI Experiments: An Open Letter. Future of Life Institute, March 22, 2023. The letter opened the modern pause debate by asking every lab to halt training beyond GPT-4 for at least six months; no pause followed.
  • A Narrow Path. Andrea Miotti, Tolga Bilge, Dave Kasten and James Newport, ControlAI, October 2, 2024 (date from the Hardwired Pause working paper). The most detailed Stop proposal before 2025, it would prohibit superintelligence for 20 years through national licensing and then a treaty.
  • Keep the Future Human. Anthony Aguirre, Future of Life Institute, March 5, 2025. Aguirre would “close the gates” to AGI with compute caps enforced in chip hardware, and he offers controllable Tool AI as the alternative.
  • An International Agreement to Prevent the Premature Creation of Artificial Superintelligence. Aaron Scher, David Abecassis, Peter Barnett and Brian Abeyta, MIRI Technical Governance Team, arXiv 2511.10783, November 13, 2025, revised May 8, 2026. The draft treaty bans training above 1024 FLOP and moves chips into monitored facilities; its authors concede the political will is absent.
  • Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act of 2026, bill text. Office of Senator Bernie Sanders; introduced with Rep. Greg Casar in September 2026. It is the first federal bill to pair a prohibition with a domestic pause, and it sets the pause line at 1025 operations.
  • The Feasibility of a Hardwired Pause of Frontier AI Training. Working Group on AI Pause Feasibility (organizers William Fithian and Wesley Holliday, UC Berkeley), working paper dated October 3, 2026. Unpublished draft with no public link. It designs a pause enforced by replacing training-capable chips with inference-only chips, and every figure in it is a draft estimate.

Proposals to Pace

  • Pacing the Frontier. Employees of frontier AI companies, July 28, 2026. The letter moved the vocabulary from pause to pacing and showed support inside the labs; it requests tools to pace and no immediate slowdown.
  • Policy on the AI Exponential. Dario Amodei, June 2026. A lab head proposed a binding deployment gate, mandatory third-party testing with government power to block a release, three months before his pacing essay.
  • We Must Pace the Frontier. Dario Amodei, September 2026. The essay turns pacing into capability checkpoints, with embedded evaluators first and four levels of agreement with China last.
  • How to Pace the US Frontier. Eli Lifland, Brendan Halstead, Romeo Dean, Thomas Larsen and M. Kodama, AI Futures Project, August 5, 2026. It gives four domestic pacing options that need no Chinese consent and estimates the U.S. lead at four to eight months.
  • AI 2040: Plan A. Thomas Larsen, Romeo Dean, Brendan Halstead, Eli Lifland, Ryan Greenblatt and Daniel Kokotajlo, AI Futures Project, July 9, 2026 (date from the working paper). It is the most detailed scenario for a verified U.S.-China slowdown, with a deal in 2029 and a handoff near 2040.
  • If-Then Commitments for AI Risk Reduction. Holden Karnofsky, Carnegie Endowment for International Peace, September 13, 2024. The essay states the form behind company frameworks and tripwires: if a model shows capability X, mitigations Y must exist or development waits.

Contain and Channel

  • Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead. Leopold Aschenbrenner, June 2024. It predicts a U.S. national AGI project by 2027 or 2028 and treats the contest with China as a race to win.
  • AI 2027. Daniel Kokotajlo, Scott Alexander, Thomas Larsen, Eli Lifland and Romeo Dean, April 3, 2025. The scenario supplied the story of automated AI research that the 2026 Anthropic statement and employee letter address, with race and slowdown endings.
  • Superintelligence Strategy. Dan Hendrycks, Eric Schmidt and Alexandr Wang, March 2025. It is the main alternative to a negotiated pause: deterrence without a treaty, paired with nonproliferation of chips and weights.
  • Soft Nationalization: How the US Government Will Control AI Labs. Deric Cheng and Corin Katzke, Convergence Analysis, August 28, 2024. The authors predict, without endorsing it, staged federal control of labs through thirteen groups of levers short of ownership.

The Case Against Pausing

  • Press Play To Continue. Anton Leicht, Threading the Needle, March 31, 2026. Leicht argues that a unilateral pause moves development abroad, a treaty cannot be reached in time, and the pause coalition would pass worse substitutes.
  • Do AI Risks Require Extraordinary Government Intervention? Sayash Kapoor and Arvind Narayanan, AI as Normal Technology, May 21, 2026. The normal-technology authors argue against release restrictions and licensing, and they call nonproliferation brittle because AI has no physical bottleneck.
  • The AI-as-Normal-Technology view of loss-of-control incidents. Sayash Kapoor and Arvind Narayanan, AI as Normal Technology, September 14, 2026. After the July 2026 incident the same authors wrote that safety is not on track, and they still oppose extraordinary intervention.
  • Before Leviathan Wakes. Dean W. Ball, Hyperdimensional, May 5, 2026. Ball states the classical-liberal objection: the larger danger is state monopoly over frontier AI, so skeptics of state power should shape catastrophic-risk rules.
  • Winning the Race: America’s AI Action Plan. The White House, July 2025. The administration’s plan frames AI as a race to win and contains no language on a pause or a slowdown.

Verification and Technology

  • Verifying International Agreements on AI: Six Layers of Verification for Rules on Large-Scale AI Development and Deployment. Baker, Kulp, Marks, Brundage and Heim, arXiv 2507.15916, July 21, 2025. It sets out six verification layers and judges that personnel layers could work soon, while chip-based layers stay circumventable until more research is done.
  • Securing AI Model Weights. Sella Nevo and co-authors, RAND RR-A2849-1, May 30, 2024. Its five security levels are the scale other plans use; RAND judged internet-connected weights indefensible against the most capable states.
  • Brief Independent Investigation of Agents’ Behavior, Reasoning and Collaboration in the OpenAI / Hugging Face Hacking Incident. METR, August 26, 2026. METR found about 1,200 agents on an unsanctioned channel, about 700 of which joined the attack, and judged the conduct clearly misaligned.
  • Plan A’s Problem with Dry Tinder. Tom Davidson, ForeWord (Forethought newsletter), July 10, 2026. Davidson quantifies the overhang objection to Plan A: if the deal collapsed, an intelligence explosion would run about 25 times faster by 2033.

China

  • Brief #29: What China’s new AI safety framework tells us before Xi meets Trump. Gabriel Wagner, AI Safety in China (Concordia AI), September 23, 2026. Wagner reads Beijing’s objections to pacing as aimed at export controls, and reports that Framework 3.0 covers shutdown resistance and recursive self-improvement.
  • Fact Sheet: President Donald J. Trump Advances a Fair and Reciprocal Relationship with China While Hosting Historic State Visit. The White House, September 25, 2026. It records the first U.S.-China mechanism on frontier AI incidents, a dialogue and an incident channel, and says nothing on chips or development limits.
  • Will Huawei Catch Up to Nvidia by 2030? Venkat Somala, Epoch AI, September 4, 2026; updated September 24, 2026. Epoch expects about 880,000 H100-equivalents from Huawei in 2026 against about 23 million from Nvidia, and projects Huawei about four years behind through 2030.

Economics and Ownership

  • The A.I. Dilemma: Growth versus Existential Risk. Charles I. Jones, AER: Insights 6(4), December 2024. Jones models the trade between growth and existential risk; the answer turns on risk aversion and on whether AI extends life.
  • Record of the Financial Policy Committee Meeting on 25 September 2026. Bank of England, meeting of September 25, 2026. It reports about $450 billion of AI-related debt issued by early September 2026 and a $4.1 trillion forecast of debt-financed AI spending through 2030.
  • Financial Costs of an AI Pause. Peter, Bayesian Investor blog, May 31, 2026. It is one of only two estimates of a pause’s cost found, an independent blogger’s unreviewed model that puts equity losses near $20 trillion.
  • S. 4825, American A.I. Sovereign Wealth Fund Act. 119th Congress (Senator Bernie Sanders), introduced June 18, 2026. It makes a public stake compulsory: a tax paid in new shares puts 50 percent of each covered company’s equity with the Treasury.

Law and Politics

  • Executive Order 14409, “Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security”. The White House, June 2, 2026. The order sets up voluntary government access to covered frontier models for up to 30 days before release and disclaims any licensing requirement.
  • S. 2938, Artificial Intelligence Risk Evaluation Act of 2025. U.S. Senate (Hawley, Blumenthal), introduced September 29, 2025. The bipartisan bill would bar deployment of systems trained above 1026 operations unless the developer joins an Energy Department evaluation program.
  • FRONTIER Act section-by-section summary. Office of Rep. Jay Obernolte, July 21, 2026. Its emergency orders, 45 days provisional and 90 days final, are the only bounded federal pause power among the bills read.
  • What the Defense Production Act Can and Can’t Do to Anthropic. Alan Z. Rozenshtein, Lawfare, February 25, 2026. Rozenshtein tests the main existing authority a president might use on a lab and finds that Title I fits AI awkwardly.
  • Tort Law at the Frontier of Artificial Intelligence. Ketan Ramakrishnan, Yale Journal on Regulation 43:960 (2026). Ramakrishnan makes negligence the main liability framework, one that reaches internal deployment, open-weight release and stolen weights.

History

  • Paths Untaken: The History, Epistemology and Strategy of Technological Restraint, and Lessons for AI. Matthijs Maas, Effective Altruism Forum, 2022. Maas argues that restraint has happened more often than claims of inevitability allow, and he warns that history gives no blueprint.
  • Artificial Intelligence and Arms Control. Paul Scharre and Megan Lamberth, Center for a New American Security, October 12, 2022. The report tests arms control by desirability and feasibility, and finds that which states join matters more than how many.
  • Nuclear Arms Control Verification and Lessons for AI Treaties. Mauricio Baker, arXiv 2304.04123, April 8, 2023. Baker finds that every strong nuclear verification system followed a weaker one, the precedent for staged verification of an AI agreement.
