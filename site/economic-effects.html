Channels: How Pacing Reaches the Economy

Any rule that slows or contains frontier AI would reach the economy through six channels. Each has measured data and contested claims about size.

  • Investment.
    • Measured. Four hyperscalers spent $171.75 billion on capital in Q2 2026 (Platformonomics).
    • Estimated. AI investment’s share of U.S. growth runs from about 0.1 percentage point (Goldman Sachs) to about 1 point (St. Louis Fed).
    • Claimed. The Hardwired Pause working paper (October 2026, unpublished draft) argues that a training moratorium cuts model-creation spending and leaves deployment spending largely intact (§6.1). An independent model assumes a 90 percent cut in training spending, partly offset by inference demand (Bayesian Investor).
  • Equity wealth.
    • Measured. The Magnificent Seven are 34.9 percent of the S&P 500 (Motley Fool Research). The U.S. household saving rate is 2.7 percent, against a 2000 to 2019 average of 5.2 percent (Flossbach von Storch).
    • Claimed. The working paper’s illustration is a $6.5 trillion fall that trims consumption by $195 billion, or 0.6 percent of GDP (§6.1). The independent model’s median is an immediate 27.8 percent fall in the S&P 500 (Bayesian Investor).
  • Credit.
    • Measured. Global AI-related debt issuance reached about $450 billion by early September 2026 (Bank of England).
    • Claimed. The working paper sees serious risk only if data center demand falls and private credit loans default (§6.1). The Bank of England says leverage and opacity could amplify losses in a correction (FPC record, September 2026).
  • Jobs.
    • Measured. Employment of workers aged 22 to 25 in the most exposed occupations trails less exposed peers by 19 percent (Stanford Digital Economy Lab). A typical data center reportedly has about 50 permanent employees (Axios).
    • Claimed. Ryan Bourne of the Cato Institute argues that disruption from existing systems would continue under a slowdown (Bourne, September 16, 2026). No analysis of the employment effect of a full stop was found.
  • Productivity.
    • Measured. Nine in ten executives in a four-country survey report no effect of AI on productivity or employment over three years (NBER Working Paper 34836).
    • Claimed. The low projection is under 0.1 percentage point of extra annual productivity growth (Acemoglu). The high projections have growth about ten times faster under broad automation (Erdil and Besiroglu).
  • Trade.
    • Measured. About 90 percent of U.S. high-tech equipment investment is sourced abroad (FEDS Notes, July 14, 2026). Taiwan’s AI-related exports to the United States reached roughly 14 percent of its GDP in Q2 2025 (FEDS Notes, February 13, 2026).
    • Claimed. The IMF says a sharp fall in technology investment would spill over to economies that export technology products (IMF, April 2026). Federal Reserve staff say U.S. external deficits would ease quickly if capital spending slowed (FEDS Notes, July 14, 2026).

How the Families Differ

The four statements below are inferences from each family’s design and the baseline data. No source states them.

  • Stop. A full stop removes training demand and tests whether inference revenue can carry the installed debt.
  • Pace. Pacing leaves inference revenue in place and lowers the option value of future models, which sits mostly in equity prices.
  • Contain and Channel. Contained development keeps training demand and chip orders. It defers the commercial revenue that services the debt unless federal contracts replace it.
  • Continue. Development without a pause or pacing rule keeps every revenue channel open and adds to the leverage that central banks have flagged.

Published analysis of these effects is very thin. Only two estimates of a pause were found, and they disagree by a wide margin. Pacing proposals have theory behind them and no estimates, and no published economic analysis of contained development was found.

One estimate is section 6.1 of the Hardwired Pause working paper, an unfinished draft that assumes inference revenue continues. The other is an independent blogger’s model of a drastic two-year pause, not peer reviewed, that reprices the whole AI revenue path. Neither models credit contagion.

No Wall Street, think tank, Council of Economic Advisers or Treasury estimate of any pause was found. The same holds for the Sanders and Casar bill (press release, September 3, 2026). The pacing proposals reviewed carry no estimate of revenue, investment, price or valuation effects (How to Pace the US Frontier; Pacing the Frontier).

No source found compares the four families on common assumptions, so most cells in the table are inferences from proposal design. The search was limited: bank client notes and industry-funded cost studies could not be searched.

Financial Stability and Credit Markets

Spending now exceeds the hyperscalers’ cash flow, and debt fills the gap. Official bodies name leverage and opacity as amplifiers of a possible correction, and none says a crisis is imminent.

Exposures

  • Measured. Global AI-related debt issuance reached about $450 billion by early September 2026, more than double the 2025 total (Bank of England).
  • Measured. Private credit outstanding to AI firms grew from near zero to over $200 billion (BIS Bulletin 120).
  • Measured, via press. Five hyperscalers had $662 billion of uncommenced lease commitments off balance sheet at end-2025 (Fortune, on a Moody’s study).
  • Measured. Bank credit commitments to nonbank financial institutions reached $2.6 trillion in Q4 2025 (Federal Reserve).
  • Measured. Private placements were 23.4 percent of U.S. insurers’ admitted bonds in 2025, up from 18.3 percent in 2021 (Insurance Business).
  • Measured. Nvidia held $42.3 billion of non-marketable equity securities on April 26, 2026 (Nvidia Form 10-Q). The BIS calls equity stakes tied to future purchase commitments circular financing (BIS).
  • Not confirmed. No source found gives insurers’ or pension funds’ dollar exposure to data center debt.

What Regulators Have Said

  • November 2025. Federal Reserve Vice Chair Jefferson called the financial system sound and named rising debt use by AI firms as a new risk (Jefferson).
  • January 2026. BIS economists judged the risks moderate. They noted that lenders price AI loans like other loans while equity markets price in high growth (BIS Bulletin 120).
  • June 2026. The BIS said hyperscaler outlays exceed earnings and free cash flow (BIS Annual Economic Report 2026).
  • September 2026. The Bank of England said the risk of a sharper correction persisted after the July fall. It said a reassessment of AI productivity gains could reach sovereign debt markets (FPC record).
  • Reported, secondhand. A Treasury staff draft reportedly likened parts of the AI market to the dot-com bubble. Treasury called the findings unvetted (summary of a NOTUS report, July 6, 2026).

Contested Points

  • How systemic. The Hardwired Pause working paper holds that equity losses alone would not be systemic (§6.1). It judges that a training freeze alone would not cause defaults if inference demand is mostly sub-frontier. The Bank of England puts more weight on exposures that are hard to see (FPC record).
  • Room to cut. The working paper says the Federal Reserve could cushion a shock from a policy rate near 4 percent. The September 2026 projections show PCE inflation at 3.7 percent (FOMC). The paper does not address that possible limit on rate cuts (inference).
  • Precedent. The 2001 recession stayed mild alongside rate cuts of 5.5 percentage points by June 2003 (Greenspan, January 3, 2004).

Mitigations Proposed

  • Hardwired Pause working paper. Monitor and limit bank lending to private credit, scrutinize private credit ownership of insurers, require disclosure on securitized private credit, and regulate lease vehicles (§6.1).
  • Two-Lane Plan. Limits on bank and insurer exposure to data center debt, long-term federal compute contracts, and resolution plans. No published analysis found tests these safeguards.
  • Insurance supervisors. The NAIC is reviewing insurers’ data center holdings. Since January 2026 it can challenge ratings more than three notches from its own view (Insurance Business).

By Family

  • Stop. The test is whether inference revenue can service debt raised against training-era demand (inference).
  • Pace. A pace that leaves inference revenue intact would test equity valuations more than debt (inference).
  • Contain and Channel. The open question is whether lenders would treat federal contracts like hyperscaler leases. No source found answers it.
  • Continue. Debt-financed AI capital spending is forecast at about $4.1 trillion for 2026 through 2030 (Bank of England).

Investment, Chips and Data Centers

A rule on frontier training would touch a minority of installed compute directly (inference). Its wider effect turns on how much other compute is bought in expectation of future frontier models, a share no source found measures.

Who Uses the Compute

  • Estimate. The five largest labs held under half of global AI compute at end-2025 (Epoch AI).
  • Estimate. Final training runs were about 10 percent of OpenAI’s R&D compute spending in 2024 (Epoch AI).
  • Estimate, from the working paper’s reference list. OpenAI and Anthropic commit 62 percent of their compute to R&D and training (SemiAnalysis).
  • Measured, one panel. Frontier models were 45 percent of token share in Ramp’s August 2026 data, down from a 53 percent peak (Ramp).
  • Estimate. Inference demand is growing about tenfold a year against supply growth of about 3.4 times (Epoch AI).

Chips

  • Measured. Nvidia’s revenue was $81.6 billion in the quarter ended April 26, 2026. Its supply commitments were $119 billion, with $95 billion due within the fiscal year (Nvidia Form 10-Q).
  • Measured. Export rules on the H20 chip led to a $4.5 billion charge for excess inventory and purchase obligations (Nvidia Form 10-K).
  • Position. The Hardwired Pause working paper expects makers of high-margin training chips to lose and other suppliers to be roughly indifferent (§6.2). No source found tests that claim.
  • Scenario. The independent model puts the immediate loss in semiconductor market value at 56.2 percent (Bayesian Investor).

Data Centers

  • Reported. About 4,000 data centers operate in the United States, 580 of them hyperscale. At least 75 projects worth about $130 billion were delayed by local politics as of Q1 2026 (Axios).
  • Measured. Data center construction added 0.07 percentage point to real GDP growth in the first nine months of 2025 (St. Louis Fed).
  • Position. The working paper argues that open-weight models on enterprise hardware may threaten data center demand more than a training freeze would (§6.1).

Overinvestment

  • Model result. An investment race yields about 1.5 times the efficient level of investment in one BIS model (BIS Working Paper 1367, abstract only).
  • Position. Federal Reserve staff note that canals and railroads ended with substantial overcapacity. They call significant AI overinvestment possible and unsettled (FEDS Notes, July 6, 2026).

By Family

  • Stop. Model-creation spending falls by definition. Data center demand holds only if inference absorbs capacity built for training (inference).
  • Pace. The AI Futures Project’s compute-floor option would hold capabilities R&D to about 5 percent of lab compute (How to Pace the US Frontier). Its authors give no investment estimate.
  • Contain and Channel. Training demand and chip orders continue inside containment (inference). The Two-Lane Plan adds federal compute contracts as an anchor.
  • Embargo Plus U.S. Pause. It adds the loss of licensed H200 sales to China, which reportedly pay 25 percent of each sale to the U.S. Treasury (Tom’s Hardware).
  • Continue. A Goldman Sachs baseline scenario reaches $1.6 trillion a year of AI capital spending by 2031 (Goldman Sachs Global Institute).

Productivity and Growth

Deployed AI has moved aggregate productivity little through mid-2026. The growth cost of a pause therefore depends on which growth model holds, and published projections span three orders of magnitude.

Measured So Far

  • Measured. U.S. labor productivity rose 2.2 percent over the four quarters to Q2 2026. Growth since Q4 2019 has equaled the average since 1947 (Bureau of Labor Statistics).
  • Measured. Nine in ten executives in a four-country survey report no effect on productivity or employment over three years (NBER Working Paper 34836).
  • Measured. Danish administrative data show precise zero effects on earnings and hours two years after ChatGPT’s release (Humlum and Vestergaard).
  • Measured. Employment of workers aged 22 to 25 in the most exposed occupations trails less exposed peers by 19 percent (Stanford Digital Economy Lab).

What a Pause Would Forgo

  • Inference. The first four projections model adoption of roughly current capabilities. On their own terms, a frontier pause leaves those gains largely in place.
  • Position. The Hardwired Pause working paper expects little reduction in productivity growth from a training freeze (§6.1). It names cheaper inference as the main near-term welfare gain (§6.2).
  • Scenario, seen only through a newspaper column. An Anthropic Institute model reportedly has GDP up 3.4 percent by 2030 with capabilities held at today’s level (Bourne).
  • Inference. Under the explosive-growth models, a multi-year pause forgoes most of the upside. The 2025 and 2026 data cannot yet tell the models apart.

By Family

  • Stop and Pace. Both keep adoption gains from existing models and defer gains that need new capabilities (inference).
  • Contain and Channel. New systems reach the public only after clearing the gate. No source found estimates the effect on growth.
  • Continue. The full range of projections stays open, along with displacement. One reported scenario from the same model pairs GDP up about 33 percent by 2030 with 18 percent knowledge-worker unemployment (Bourne). Goldman Sachs’ chief economist expects unemployment in ten years near the 4.1 percent of July 2026 (Goldman Sachs).
  • Pacing adoption. One model finds that slowing automation raises welfare about 4 percent when displaced workers adjust slowly (Beraja and Zorzi). The Two-Lane Plan’s option to pace adoption in high-displacement sectors has no estimate behind it.

Global Economy

A fall in U.S. AI hardware demand would reach Taiwan and Korea first, through trade. Financial stress would travel through foreign holdings of U.S. equities and through bond markets where hyperscalers are now large issuers.

Supplier Economies

  • Taiwan. Measured. AI-related exports to the United States reached roughly 14 percent of Taiwan’s GDP in Q2 2025 (FEDS Notes, February 13, 2026). TSMC fabricates nearly the entire global supply of AI chips (Epoch AI).
  • Korea. Measured. Korea grew at a 7.5 percent annual rate in Q1 2026, and the IMF raised its 2026 forecast to 2.6 percent (IMF, July 2026). Samsung and SK hynix make high-bandwidth memory, the tightest constraint on AI chips as of late 2025 (Epoch AI).
  • Japan and the Netherlands. Not confirmed. No source found sizes either country’s macro exposure. ASML of the Netherlands is the sole maker of extreme ultraviolet lithography machines (Epoch AI). SoftBank of Japan reportedly committed $30 billion to OpenAI’s March 2026 round (Yahoo Finance).
  • Other exporters. Measured. The top four AI hardware exporters beat growth forecasts by an average of 4.4 percentage points in Q1 2026 (IMF, July 2026). They are Taiwan, Korea, Thailand and Malaysia.
  • The mirror image. Goldman Sachs’ chief economist said in February 2026 that much U.S. AI spending raises Taiwanese and Korean GDP (Gizmodo, via The Wealth Advisor, secondary). A U.S. cut would then subtract more from their output than from U.S. output (inference).

Other Advanced Economies

  • Position. Foreign ownership of U.S. equities has grown, so a sharp U.S. correction would cause sizable wealth losses abroad (IMF Blog).
  • Measured. AI hyperscalers accounted for 47 percent of sterling corporate bond issuance in 2026 through early September (Bank of England).
  • Scenario. A moderate correction in AI stock valuations lowers global growth by 0.4 percentage point (IMF, January 2026). The scenario concerns a market correction and does not model a pause.

Developing Countries

  • Estimate, 2024. AI exposure covers about 60 percent of jobs in advanced economies and 26 percent in low-income countries (IMF Blog, January 14, 2024). The IMF’s Managing Director warned that the technology could widen gaps between countries.
  • Position. The World Bank reports a trend toward “small AI,” affordable applications that run on everyday devices (World Bank, landing page only).
  • Reported. A Bloomberg report on remarks by Treasury Secretary Bessent says Chinese open-weight models nearly match U.S. models at lower cost (Bloomberg, via Yahoo Finance).
  • Inference. Cheaper inference on existing models, which the Hardwired Pause working paper predicts (§6.2), would favor late adopters. No source found says whether a frontier pause would narrow or widen the gap.

By Family

  • Stop. Taiwan and Korea lose orders for training-class chips and memory (inference).
  • Embargo Plus U.S. Pause. The plan would also end the November 2025 truce that paused export controls (IMF, January 2026). The truce was due to expire in November 2026. On September 24, 2026 the United States announced an extension of about two months, to January 10 (Bloomberg via Stars and Stripes). China mined about 69 percent of world rare earth output in 2025 (USGS).
  • Pace, and Contain and Channel. No published analysis found. Chip orders would continue under contained development (inference).
  • Continue. Supplier economies keep their export gains and their exposure to a correction (inference).
  • All families. No source found models spillovers from a policy-induced U.S. AI slowdown to supplier economies.

The Economics of Delay: Growth Against Risk

Formal economics on trading growth against catastrophic risk is thin and recent. Its answers turn on risk aversion and on whether AI extends life, and no paper found prices a pause of defined length.

Carlsmith (2026)Background mortality 0.75 percent a year; other existential risk 0.1 percentMulti-year pause pays only if it cuts alignment risk 0.1 to 1 percent yearly.

What Each Side Draws From the Models

  • For a pause. Under standard risk aversion, the 2024 Jones model accepts about 4 percent total existential risk for consumption gains (Jones 2024). His 2025 paper finds mitigation spending of at least 1 percent of GDP justified in most scenarios examined.
  • Against a pause. If AI halves mortality, the risk Jones’s model tolerates rises about tenfold. Bostrom counts about 170,000 deaths a day without the technology (Bostrom). Carlsmith lists costs of restraint that include deaths during delay and ground ceded to authoritarian states.
  • A benchmark. The United States gave up about 4 percent of GDP in the pandemic to address a mortality risk near 0.3 percent (Jones 2025).
  • The value of health gains. Murphy and Topel valued a 1 percent cut in cancer mortality at nearly $500 billion in 2005 dollars (NBER Working Paper 11405). No source found estimates AI’s effect on the pace of medical progress.

Bearing on the Families

  • Stop. Jones’s 2025 paper concerns mitigation budgets and does not model a pause. A pause spends through forgone output, so the pandemic figure serves only as a benchmark (inference).
  • Pace. Acemoglu and Lensman support gradual adoption, and Gans supports faster adoption where use reveals harms. Beraja and Zorzi give the nearest formal support for pacing adoption in high-displacement sectors (inference).
  • Contain and Channel. If harms are learned in contained testing, as the Two-Lane Plan assumes, the Gans objection weakens (inference). Neither paper models contained development. Bostrom’s advice has the same shape: move fast to capability, then pause briefly before full deployment.
  • Continue. The mortality result and Bostrom’s lifespan arithmetic are formal support this family can cite (inference).

Open Questions

No published work found estimates the economic effect of pacing or of contained development, and neither named plan has been modeled. The tasks start with that modeling and end with checks on secondhand figures.
