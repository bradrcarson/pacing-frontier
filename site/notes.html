Instructions for Contributing Agents

These rules keep the document consistent as more agents add to it.

Entry Template

Each proposal entry uses the same fields in the same order.

  1. Source. Authors, organization, date, link.
  2. What It Does. Two to four sentences in the proponents’ own terms.
  3. How It Works. What is restricted, who is bound, the trigger, the exit, and how compliance is checked.
  4. Pros. The strongest arguments for it, attributed.
  5. Cons. The strongest arguments against it, attributed.
  6. Economy. Expected effects on the U.S. and global economy.
  7. Ownership. What it implies for who owns and controls the AI companies.
  8. Questions. Open questions specific to this proposal, written as tasks.

Evidence Rules

  • Open the source. Cite only what you have opened and read. Do not cite from memory.
  • Tag every source. Opened means you read it. Listed means it appeared in a search and you did not open it. Paper ref means it comes from another paper’s reference list.
  • Say when you could not confirm. Write “Not confirmed” and what you searched.
  • Attribute. Separate what proponents claim, what critics claim and what is established.
  • Date everything. The debate moved quickly in 2026, and older sources may be out of date.

House Style

  • Title Case headings.
  • Curly quotation marks and apostrophes.
  • No em dashes.
  • No “not X but Y” and no “X rather than Y” constructions.
  • No sentence that ends in a trailing “-ing” phrase.
  • Few three-part lists.
  • No signposting phrases and no asides about candor.
  • Sentences under 25 words. Lead each section with its point.

Working in This Document

  • Add questions as tasks. Use the checklist format so that items can be ticked off.
  • Answer a standard question by its identifier. Write “H3:” before the answer so that it can be found.
  • Leave other contributors’ text in place. Add a dated note beneath it if you disagree.
  • Log each session. Add a line to the workplan saying what you changed.
